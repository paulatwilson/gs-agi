GS/AGI demo: asset mockups round 4

Drawn with what the engine will really have: 320×200 pixels, 16 colours from a 12-bit palette, an 8×8 font, vector shapes. New this round: the pond in the clearing is alive. The water shimmers, ripples spread across it, and every few seconds a fish leaps out with a splash and a small plip. Open room 2 and wait.

The two screens

Click the screen, then walk with the arrow keys (or the pad). She gets smaller toward the horizon and her steps shorten with her. Click or press a key once to start the audio. Room 2 has the pond (browsers wait for that). Try the rock puzzle below, then walk off the east edge of the meadow into the clearing.

Try the rock puzzle (meadow)
  1. look rock to see it is loose.
  2. move rock while standing near it. Try it from across the screen first to see the “too far” reply.
  3. The rock slides left with a dragging sound and leaves a hole. It blocks walking in its new spot.
  4. look hole to look in. The coins appear in the hole.
  5. get coins to pick them up, then inventory.

The heroine

24×48 pixels with a black outline, a dress with a defined waist and flared skirt, long hair, and shading in a second dress tone and a skin shadow. That is three times the pixel count of the first sketch, which only the Super Hi-Res screen can carry cleanly. Four directions, three poses each (stand, left foot, right foot); the walk plays stand, left, stand, right. Left is the right-facing art mirrored. Shown at 4×.

Perspective scaling

She scales with her distance up the screen, from 50% at the horizon line to 100% at the bottom edge. The same 24×48 art is resampled at draw time, so it costs no extra memory. Shown at 3× for the sizes she will actually have on the real screen, feet on a common line.

Her walking step shrinks with her (3 pixels at full size, about 1.5 at half), so she doesn’t seem to glide faster when far away. Trees and rocks stay a fixed size.

Palette

16 colours as IIgs 12-bit values ($0RGB). Re-planned for her: dress shade and gold are new, and the old shallow water and light foliage colours were folded into sky and meadow green to make room. Black is index 0 and white is 15, which the text and boxes assume.

How the rock mechanic works in the engine

Nothing new is needed in the VM. The rock is an animated object, not part of the background picture, so it can move. The hole is a small vector picture laid over the background when the rock moves. The same flags and distance check that the rest of the game uses decide what you can do.

  • Rock as an object. A view with its own position. move.obj slides it 32 pixels, and its walk-blocking footprint moves with it.
  • Hole as an overlay. overlay.pic draws a tiny picture (two ellipses and a rim) when flag rock_moved is set. It is saved with the room state.
  • Reach check. distance(ego, rock, v): if v is over 46 the reply is “You are too far away.”
  • Coins. look hole sets coins_seen and draws the coins into the hole. get coins checks the reach, calls get(gold_coins), erases them and adds 5 to the score. Looking again says it is empty.
  • Sound. move rock also calls sound(drag, f_drag_done). The ambient tune is started by LOGIC.0 and restarted whenever its done-flag is set, which is how AGI loops music.
if (said(move, rock)) {
  if (isset(rock_moved)) { print("You already moved it."); }
  else {
    distance(ego, rock, v50);
    if (v50 > 46) { print("You are too far away."); }
    else {
      set(rock_moved);
      move.obj(rock, 80, 168, 2, f_rock_done);
      overlay.pic(hole);
      print("You heave the rock aside. It leaves a shallow hole in the ground.");
    }
  }
}

Pond life

Room 2 only. Eight water glints cycle through three frames, a ripple ring spreads every couple of seconds, and at random intervals (about 4 to 9 seconds) a fish leaps up to 24 pixels out of the water, turns over and splashes back in. The fish has three poses, shown below at 4× and mirrored when it jumps left. Every moving piece is an ordinary animated object, so it costs nothing new in the engine and runs the same on the IIgs and on the Mac.

  • Glints: 8 objects, view of 3 cels (small line, long line, pale line), cycle_time 4 cycles, each starting on a different cel so the water never pulses in step.
  • Ripples: a ring view of 9 cels (expanding, white then pale blue) drawn at a random pond position by the room logic; fish take-off and landing add one each.
  • Fish: one object. A countdown variable runs while you are in the room; at zero the logic picks a start point and a direction with random, then plays four move.obj legs (up, apex, down, landing) while it sets the cel to rising, level or falling.
  • Sound: sound(splash, f_splash_done) at take-off. One effect covers the whole jump: a small splash, a gap while it is in the air, then a landing splash and a short rising plip.
// LOGIC.2, once per cycle
if (v70 > 0) { --v70; }                  // countdown to next jump
if (v70 == 0 && !isset(f_fish_up)) {
  random(150, 230, v71);                 // start x
  random(142, 151, v72);                 // start y
  random(0, 1, v73);                     // direction
  set(f_fish_up);
  sound(splash, f_splash_done);
  ...position(fish, v71, v72), then 4 move.obj legs...
}
if (isset(f_fish_done)) { reset(f_fish_up); erase(fish); random(60, 135, v70); }

Sound

Both sounds are note lists: pitch, volume and length in 1/60 s ticks, grouped into generators, exactly the SOUND.n layout the engine already reads. On the IIgs each generator is an Ensoniq DOC oscillator; on the Mac it is a small software oscillator. This page plays them with the same sequencing rule as the engine’s sound.c, so what you hear is the arrangement that ships. The real hardware will sound plainer than this browser synth, which smooths note starts and ends.

SoundKindGeneratorsNotesLengthFile size

Music uses generators 0 to 3 and effects use 4 and 5, so a sound effect never cuts the music off. Effects share one slot, so a splash replaces a drag still playing. The format gains one byte for this (kind: effect or music), and one more waveform id for noise. Waveform ids: 0 square, 1 triangle, 2 sawtooth, 3 noise, 4 sine.
